above all the reasons i hate art, i think i can trace the key issue. the key issue is that i, of all people, strove toward it. i had a pull towards art even though i was bad at it, and for some reason, whoever controls these things - the things of one's interests and skills - decided that it would be a good joke to make someone who was attracted to art but had no skill for it. what kind of cruelty is that? and hyksos says there are higher, benevolent powers. if they are so benevolent, then they ought to explain to me why they have given me a laughable way of life, the artist who could never be. no amount of his philosophy ever seems to explain it.

and maybe that's the true reason i hate art. i hate art because there's something inside of me that likes it, even when the world has told me that i don't have the talent nor the potential for artistry. so be it, then. science welcomes everyone, even the fool, so long as they are willing to learn.

my journey to science stemmed from my misadventure with art. the dissatisfaction, frustration, lack of societal respect, all factors which drove me away from art - they were not to be found as much in the realm of science.

we call people einsteins after the famous scientist, and when we do so, we are giving them a sort of respect, a sort of jealousy even. it's as if we could never fully understand the einsteins of the world, so just like how we crown artists with a title, so too do we crown the einsteins. but unlike the artist who is laughed at until centuries later, often the scientist can avoid this experience and can be respected among contemporary audiences. such is the beauty of science. and, of course, science is more practically useful than any painting. medicine, computer science, engineering - these are all essential sciences for the good life in the modern world and for the sustainment of more developed economies. and at the centre of these essential sciences, i saw mathematics.
