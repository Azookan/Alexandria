it's become evident to me that despite my attempts to tell myself why i hate art, i cannot simply blame one bad experience. it is really not one bad experience, but a collection of bad experiences which have led me to think that in one way or another, the powers that be are not allowing me to be an artist.

aside from that incident from high school, there were the other things - like my parents hovering over me, saying that the artist's life is a life without money. art's future, to them, is a future of nothing. it is not just the absence of colour, but it is the non-existence of colour. it is the non-existence of the self. after all, how many artists are there who have not benefited from their own work until centuries later, when somehow the world has come to look at their brilliance? even then, we treat their sweat and struggles as a tourist attraction, only one photo and nothing more. that's all, folks! that's all it'll ever be.

it reminds me of a book i read from walter benjamin. it says that in our advancements of technology, the aura of art has decayed. that enchanting thing, that otherworldly quality, that value of art... it is gone, but not because the art has gotten old. no, it is because now, anyone can have art. take a picture of your favourite painting, and you'll have a copy of it forever - after all, how can art ever be magical when it can be recreated over and over again, until it is part of the everyday? what value would i give a concert if i could just get a recording of it on my phone? thus, art has become cheap, boring, run-of-the-mill, oversaturated; but of course, it has always been cheap, boring, etc. to my parents regardless of benjamin's insights. my parents were one reason i could never become an artist.
