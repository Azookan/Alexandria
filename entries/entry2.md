Anime and video games are a form of escapism for some, and I guess it's true. But I don't care too much - like, let's be real for a moment. If you think that the point of life is to have fun, then a bit of escapism isn't just okay. Escapism is the point! Now, if you think that the point of life is something greater, escapism is still a tool to unwind. Even then, if you want, you don't have to think of these things as escapism-related. You can think of them as little simulations, simulations of the world. Like, imagine what it would be like to be Kaito. You just get dropped into a new world out of nowhere. How would you survive? These are the kinds of questions which are fun to think about, and they also reveal a bit about ourselves, I think. They help us think about what kind of people we want to be and what thought processes we would use in certain situations.

To make an analogy to math: we solve problems where Charlie buys 99 chocolate bars, but it's all in preparation for more important questions. For example, when Charlie buys 99 chocolate bars and eats 4, we ask how many bars are left; it helps us wrap our minds around the idea of subtraction. Well, we need subtraction if we need to start talking about debt in finance. So, even if a toy situation is really fake, it can still be helpful. that's the point I'm making with anime and video games.
