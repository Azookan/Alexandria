Cake is a funny thing. I like cake, especially black forest cake - THE CAKE IS A LIE! - but there are also funny things about cake when you think about math. Let's say, for example, you wanted to figure out the volume of a cake, but you didn't know the volume of a cylinder (because most cakes are cylinders). That's totally fine. What you could do is just cut the cake into a bunch of tiny triangles before putting the triangles together so that they look like one big parallelogram. Now you've just turned your cake into something that's very close to a parallelogram-faced prism - and you can calculate the volume of that!

While that may sound like a very nerdy and obscure example, this was basically an instance of calculus. There's a funny little thing that happens when you try and take smaller and smaller versions of things like cake slices: the outer curves of the cake start to mellow out and appear like straight lines. From there, you can construct the parallelogram. It's kind of hard to explain just with words, but the point still stands. We're talking about things that happen as things change, as the cake slices get smaller and smaller. In short, we might say that cake is cool since it's related to calculus and cheerful birthday parties! (I was trying to be alliterative; did that come through?)
