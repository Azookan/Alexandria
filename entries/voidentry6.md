it is a strange thing for me to enjoy math when so many despise it. i feel like that in some way, i have been tangling with forces the likes of which are powerful and yet capricious. there is no doubt that math just happens to be based on the assumptions we make. we both create and explore math, isn't that the case?

and here, i should explain. some people think we only create math, but that, to me, seems awkward. i can create an argument for why our country should do x, but we discover the consequences of x once our country actually implements x. we do not create the consequences. in that same fashion, i can choose to believe that 1 is the next whole number after 0; but i need to discover the consequences of that tiny choice.

secondly, some people think that we only discover math, but i think that's a bad way to think of math. if we choose the things to believe in and if we create the things we want in math - mathematicians do it all the time - then how exactly do we discover math? if anything, we are discovering what are minds are capable of, but that's not the same thing as the discovery of math itself.

based on these assertions, it seems clear to me that math is both created and discovered. and yet, someone might accuse me of being an artist. but that isn't the case here. the act of creation is not necessarily the act of art. let's say i made a hole in the sand to bury treasure. i created the hole, nobody would say that the hole is art. in the same way, we may create mathematics, but mathematics is not art; it is a science. it is a formal science, but it is a science nonetheless. it has that title of science which fits it much better than the subjectivity and cheapness of art. i can't betray myself in saying that, can i?
